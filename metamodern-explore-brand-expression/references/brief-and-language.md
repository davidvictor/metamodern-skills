# Brief and language

How to write the brand facts in the PRESERVE block and every concept, thesis, and tension in the sheets. Carried from the earlier visual-direction method where it still holds.

## Evidence-first hypothesis

Begin from the accepted platform and current sources rather than an interview. State only the parts the sheet needs: what the organisation is, who decides and in what context, the position it can credibly occupy, its character in conduct words, and the central tension. Ask one focused question only when the answer would change the sheet set or the preserve block.

## Translate abstract language

Turn broad attributes into observable choices before they enter a sheet. For each word (smart, human, bold, premium, technical), resolve which meaning is intended, what visual behaviour would show it, which category convention the word usually triggers, and what tension keeps the interpretation specific. Bold may mean scale contrast rather than more colour; technical may mean engineered precision without science-fiction motifs; premium may come from restraint and material care rather than luxury codes.

## Concept language

Every thesis, tension, and subject is stated in plain speech the client would recognise as a description of their own company. Three checks: anchored to a checkable fact from the evidence; plain enough to carry in an ordinary sentence with no coined phrases; pointable, with one or two real examples the client can react to. After a correction, present the replacement alone, stated in positive terms.

## Register in conduct words

Describe how the brand behaves (blunt, still, exact, warmly challenging, quietly formidable), never how the image should feel (beautiful, premium, professional). Conduct words survive translation into colour roles and type behaviour; mood adjectives regress to the model's average.
