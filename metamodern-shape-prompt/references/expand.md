# Expand

Develop a seed into three meaningfully different, standalone prompts unless another count is requested. Go beyond paraphrasing to deepen the purpose, subject, or context while preserving the central aim.

Decide whether the request calls for alternative directions to choose between or complementary perspectives on one task. Say which relationship the options have when it affects how they can be used. Do not force alternatives into compatible parts or make every exploration a sequence of phases.

Each direction should contribute a useful question, distinction, connection, or implication supported by the context. Possible angles include the underlying problem, competing explanations, a broader purpose, consequences, or practical application. Choose angles for this thought; do not impose the same categories every time.

Keep the original facts, constraints, exclusions, voice, and uncertainty in every prompt where they matter. Make consequential assumptions visible inside the copy-ready text. A proposed audience, explanation, or deliverable must not read as an established requirement. Do not invent project facts, budgets, deadlines, platforms, research findings, or commercial terms.

For each numbered option return:

- A short, subject-specific title.
- One sentence explaining the direction and any consequential interpretive leap.
- One complete prompt, typically one to three substantive paragraphs.

Return the requested options without a preamble or sales pitch. Three tone variants or longer versions of the same instruction do not count. When the user selects a direction, develop that one instead of regenerating alternatives. Perform the underlying work only after a separate explicit execution request.
