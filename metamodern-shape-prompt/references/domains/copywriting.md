# Copywriting

Use this reference when a thought concerns language intended to help a particular audience understand, value, or act on an offer, idea, product, or service. Clarify preserves the existing message and scope. Expand develops distinct persuasive directions. Combine keeps selected claims, evidence, and constraints traceable. Prepare makes the chosen writing brief usable without inventing facts or permissions.

## Boundaries

Copywriting concerns the message a reader receives. It is not automatically marketing strategy, interface design, or prose editing. Use [Marketing](marketing.md) for audience, positioning, channels, and measurement. Use [UI Design](ui-design.md) when the task concerns a product interface helping someone complete a task rather than persuasion. Use `metamodern-refine-writing` when the supplied material is existing reader-facing prose that needs editing. A prompt that instructs an agent to write copy remains here.

## Audience, proposition, and action

An audience is the people the message is for; reader context is the situation in which they encounter it. A proposition is the central value or meaning the message asks the reader to understand. An offer is what is available on stated terms. A benefit is a valuable outcome; a feature is a characteristic or capability. Proof requires relevant evidence. Examples and a clear mechanism may explain or support a claim without establishing it. A call to action (CTA) tells the reader what they can do next.

"Make the value clearer" might mean sharpen the proposition, name a relevant benefit, order the existing information, or make the next step more explicit. It does not establish that a new offer, promise, or audience should be invented.

"Make it convert" may mean help the right reader understand an existing offer and choose a stated next action. It does not prove a conversion problem, select a metric, or authorize a claim about results.

## Voice, tone, and message hierarchy

Voice is the recognizable character of an organization or author across contexts. Tone is how that voice responds to a particular reader, circumstance, and degree of confidence. Message hierarchy is the order in which the reader receives the proposition, supporting information, proof, details, and action.

"Make it bolder" could mean more direct language, a clearer point of view, more confident sentence structure, or a stronger opening. Keep the factual support and degree of certainty intact. "Make it sound like us" requires available examples or stated voice attributes; do not invent a brand personality.

An objection is a reason a reader may hesitate. It differs from a question, a requirement, or a factual error. Address an objection only when it is supplied or clearly proposed as a direction; do not manufacture anxieties to fill a formula.

## Translate rough requests

| Rough expression | Possible precise meanings to keep open |
| --- | --- |
| "Say why this matters." | Clarify the reader-relevant proposition, benefit, or consequence. |
| "Make it more persuasive." | Improve relevance, specificity, proof, message order, or the CTA. |
| "Lead with the benefit." | Begin with a supported reader outcome before features or process detail. |
| "It feels too salesy." | Reduce pressure, unsupported certainty, or exaggerated language; the desired degree of persuasion remains open. |
| "We need stronger proof." | Identify evidence already available or formulate a request to gather it; do not fabricate testimonials, metrics, or outcomes. |
| "Give people a reason to act." | Make a legitimate next step and its relevance clear; do not add false urgency or scarcity. |

These are candidate interpretations, not automatic rewrites. A phrase may name more than one problem at once.

## Questions for expansion

Choose questions that open real alternatives from the supplied context:

- What does this audience already know, need, or risk misunderstanding at this moment?
- Is the key task to make an existing proposition clearer, surface relevant proof, or address a known hesitation?
- Which supported benefit matters most to the intended reader, and which detail earns belief?
- What action is actually available now, and what should the reader expect after taking it?
- Does the requested tone preserve the author's voice and the truthfulness of the claim?

Possible directions may differ by message emphasis, reader context, evidence order, or action framing. Do not default to AIDA, a particular audience segment, length, channel, slogan, or CTA structure unless the user supplies it or asks to explore it.

## Across prompt modes

Clarify can turn "make this clearer" into a precise request while retaining the supplied audience, claim, and voice. Expand can offer distinct directions such as proposition-first, proof-first, or objection-aware, provided each remains grounded in the same material. Combine can preserve selected benefits, evidence, and tone choices without silently dropping a qualification. Prepare can state the intended reader, source material, constraints on claims, desired artifact, and what evidence a later writer should use.

These modes produce prompts about copywriting. Writing or publishing the final copy requires a separate execution request. Keep the requested output separate from an inferred campaign or redesign.

## Evidence boundaries and sources

Keep a supplied fact, claim, result, quote, testimonial, comparison, and forecast distinct. A variant may change emphasis or order, but it cannot turn an aspiration into an observed outcome. Preserve qualifications, availability limits, and uncertainty. Do not create endorsements, customer stories, performance figures, guarantees, or legal/compliance claims.
