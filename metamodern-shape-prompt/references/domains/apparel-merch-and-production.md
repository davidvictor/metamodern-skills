# Apparel, Merch, and Production Language

Use this reference only when the dictated thought concerns apparel, merchandise, soft goods, textiles, garment development, manufacturing, or the business that supports them. It supplies classification and vocabulary for clarification, and relevant distinctions for expansion. It does not turn the request into a design specification, tech pack, sourcing plan, costing model, or production plan.

## Work areas

| Work area | Use when the intent concerns |
| --- | --- |
| Apparel and merchandise design | Concept, product category, silhouette, proportion, fit intent, colorway, graphic application, collection coherence, or the designed customer experience |
| Merchandising and line planning | Assortment, range architecture, SKU mix, drop strategy, pricing tiers, channel mix, inventory intent, or how products work together commercially |
| Technical development | Technical flats, measurements, specifications, tech packs, bills of materials, patterns, grading, fit corrections, samples, or factory-ready translation |
| Materials and textiles | Fiber, yarn, fabric, knit or woven structure, weight, hand, drape, body, stretch, recovery, surface, finish, performance, or material suitability |
| Construction, decoration, and finishing | Seams, stitches, cut-and-sew assembly, knitting, tailoring, draping, bonding, welding, closures, trims, printing, embroidery, dyeing, washing, or embellishment |
| Sourcing, costing, and production | Mills, material and trim suppliers, factories, vendor capabilities, minimums, lead times, yields, costs, capacity, production runs, reorders, or production sequencing |
| Quality, compliance, and operations | Tolerances, inspection, testing, restricted substances, certifications, licensing requirements, packaging, inventory, fulfillment, logistics, wholesale, retail, or e-commerce operations |

Classify by the decision, not by the garment or product. A hoodie discussion can be apparel design, technical development, textile selection, construction engineering, production planning, merchandising, or several of these.

## Design and development boundaries

### Apparel design vs product development

- **Apparel design** defines the intended product: concept, silhouette, proportion, details, color, surface treatment, and wearer experience.
- **Apparel product development** turns that intent into a repeatable product through specifications, materials, patterns, samples, costing, sourcing, and production decisions.
- **Technical design** manages measurable fit, construction, specifications, tolerances, and factory communication.

Use the narrowest supported term. Do not label a visual or experiential comment as technical development unless the user is discussing how to realize or control it.

### Patternmaking, grading, and fit

- **Patternmaking** creates the shaped pieces that produce the garment form.
- **Grading** increases or decreases an approved base pattern across a size range.
- **Fit development** evaluates how the garment sits, moves, balances, and relates to the intended body and size specification.
- **Ease** is the difference between body measurement and garment measurement. It can support movement, layering, comfort, or silhouette.
- **Silhouette** is the overall visible shape. **Proportion** describes relationships among its parts.

More room in one area is not automatically grading, and a fit concern is not automatically a pattern correction. Preserve the observation when the cause is unknown.

### Technical artifacts and samples

- A **technical flat** is a diagrammatic garment drawing that communicates panels, seams, details, and construction.
- A **tech pack** is the controlled factory communication package for specifications, construction, materials, trims, colorways, artwork, labeling, packaging, and revision state.
- A **bill of materials (BOM)** lists the materials, trims, components, quantities, and related specifications required for the product.
- A **prototype sample** explores the product concept or construction.
- A **fit sample** supports fit evaluation and correction.
- A **pre-production sample** confirms the approved production intent before the bulk run.

Name a sample stage only when the user supplies enough context. Do not silently advance an early concept into a factory-ready artifact.

## Materials and textiles

### Fabric, textile, and material

- **Textile** is the broad category of fiber-, yarn-, fabric-, or sheet-based material systems.
- **Fabric** is a formed textile surface, commonly woven, knitted, nonwoven, felted, bonded, or otherwise constructed.
- **Material** is broader and can include fabric, leather, synthetic sheet goods, foam, hardware, thread, interlining, labels, and packaging.

### Material properties

- **Fiber content** identifies constituent fibers, not performance by itself.
- **Fabric weight** is a measured mass, often expressed as GSM or ounces per square yard. Do not infer a number from words such as heavy or lightweight.
- **Hand** is the tactile impression of the material.
- **Drape** is how the material hangs and falls under gravity.
- **Body** is the material's ability to hold volume or shape.
- **Stiffness** is resistance to bending; it is not the same as body or structure.
- **Stretch** is extension under force. **Recovery** is the ability to return toward the original dimension.
- **Dimensional stability** concerns shrinkage, growth, twisting, or other size and shape change through wear and care.
- **Surface** concerns visible and tactile character. **Finish** is a treatment applied to alter appearance, hand, performance, or behavior.

When users describe a desired feeling, translate it into supported properties without assigning unspoken specifications. "Substantial but not stiff" may indicate weight, body, drape, hand, or a combination; keep the distinction open unless the user resolves it.

## Construction, decoration, and finishing

- **Construction** describes how components are shaped and assembled into the product.
- **Fabrication** can describe how a material or component is formed, such as knitting, weaving, bonding, welding, molding, laser cutting, or 3D printing.
- **Decoration** applies graphics or ornament, such as screen printing, digital printing, embroidery, appliqué, patches, beading, or hardware.
- **Finishing** alters the product or material after primary formation or assembly, such as garment dyeing, washing, distressing, coating, pressing, or surface treatment.
- **Trim** is a functional or decorative component such as a zipper, button, snap, drawcord, label, elastic, rib, or binding.

Cut-and-sew, knit-to-shape, tailoring, draping, bonding, and seamless construction are not interchangeable methods. Use a named method only when the speaker identifies it or the described constraint clearly depends on it.

## Sourcing, costing, and production

- **Material sourcing** identifies and qualifies fibers, fabrics, trims, components, mills, and suppliers.
- **Product costing** estimates the cost to make the product under stated assumptions.
- **Production planning** coordinates approvals, capacity, quantities, materials, timing, and manufacturing sequence.
- **Manufacturing** performs the physical making and finishing.
- **Quality assurance** establishes the system intended to prevent defects. **Quality control** inspects and tests products or processes against requirements.
- **MOQ** is a supplier or factory minimum order quantity. It is not the same as the preferred initial buy or production run.
- **Lead time** is elapsed time under defined conditions; it is not a promise unless the source and start point are known.

### Cost and commercial boundaries

- **Product cost** covers the stated cost basis for the item and may or may not include decoration, packaging, or development.
- **Landed cost** includes the stated costs required to bring goods to the receiving destination, often including product, packaging, freight, duties, and related charges.
- **Fulfilled cost** may add pick, pack, payment, storage, and last-mile fulfillment costs when those are in scope.
- **Wholesale price** is the selling price to a retailer or distributor.
- **Retail price** is the customer-facing selling price.
- **Gross margin** compares selling revenue with the defined cost of goods basis. It is not the same as markup or net profit.

State the cost basis before using a margin term. Do not turn "leave enough room" into a numeric margin target.

## Merch and business boundaries

- **Merch design** commonly means designing branded merchandise products or graphics.
- **Merchandising** plans the assortment, quantities, pricing, presentation, timing, and channels intended to meet customer and business goals.
- A **collection** is a conceptually related product group. A **line plan** organizes planned styles, categories, colorways, prices, timing, and quantities.
- A **style** is one product design. A **colorway** is a color or material variation. An **SKU** is a sellable inventory variant, often at the size and color level.
- A **drop** is a coordinated release. A limited drop can describe customer perception, production quantity, availability window, or replenishment policy; preserve which meaning the user intends.
- **Fulfillment** processes orders through pick, pack, ship, delivery, and returns. **Logistics** coordinates the broader movement and storage of goods. Neither is manufacturing.

Merchandise can include apparel, headwear, bags, accessories, home and lifestyle goods, packaging, and hardgoods. Do not classify every merch request as apparel.

## Translation patterns

| Rough expression | Possible precise language when context supports it |
| --- | --- |
| "Heavy but not stiff" | Substantial weight or hand with body and controlled drape, without excessive stiffness |
| "The hood should stand up" | Hood structure and shape retention supported by material, pattern, interlining, or construction; keep the cause open |
| "The cuffs get loose" | Rib or elastic recovery and dimensional stability through wear and laundering |
| "More room but not baggy" | Additional ease or shaping in the named area while preserving the intended silhouette |
| "The pocket sticks out" | Pocket opening gaping or flare; the cause may involve pattern, stabilization, construction, or fit |
| "The fabric should flow but keep shape" | Drape balanced with enough body and recovery to maintain the silhouette |
| "Start small and reorder" | Controlled initial production run with a replenishment decision tied to observed demand |
| "Leave room after all the costs" | Price and margin evaluation against a clearly defined product, landed, or fulfilled cost basis |
| "Make it feel limited" | Scarcity may come from quantity, availability window, distribution, editioning, or replenishment policy; preserve the intended mechanism |

These are candidate translations, not automatic specifications.

## Ambiguity and invention boundary

Do not invent fiber content, fabric weight, construction details, tolerances, MOQ, lead time, costs, margins, certifications, or production quantities.

Keep these distinctions open when the dictated thought does not resolve them:

- desired appearance vs required construction method;
- fit observation vs pattern correction;
- tactile preference vs measurable fabric specification;
- limited-edition positioning vs production quantity;
- product cost vs landed or fulfilled cost;
- decoration method vs the visual effect it should create;
- sustainability preference vs verified certification or chain of custody;
- sample feedback vs production approval.

Use one concise question only when the unresolved distinction would materially change the pass-forward statement. Otherwise preserve it as a hypothesis or named constraint.


## Questions for expansion

Choose only what serves the seed: How should the product feel and behave on a person? Which material properties could account for that experience? Is a sample comment about appearance, fit, durability, or manufacturing repeatability? How do assortment and replenishment choices relate to the intended meaning of a limited release?

Keep desired effects separate from possible ways to achieve them. Consider samples, comparisons, or unanswered questions as optional directions; do not turn every material preference into a tech pack or sourcing assignment.
