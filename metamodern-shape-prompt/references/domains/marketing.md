# Marketing

Use this reference when a thought concerns how an offer reaches, serves, and learns from a market: audience, positioning, channels, customer behavior, or measurement. Clarify retains the stated objective and uncertainty. Expand develops distinct strategic questions or directions. Combine preserves selected contributions and tensions. Prepare makes a chosen intention ready to guide later work without selecting tactics, spending, or claims.

## Boundaries

Marketing connects an offering with a market and customer behavior. It is broader than copywriting or promotion. Use [Copywriting](copywriting.md) for the message itself. Use Business and Operations for costs, prices, commercial authority, organizational ownership, or agreements. This reference helps shape a prompt; it does not conduct research, launch a campaign, collect data, change tracking, or contact an audience.

## Market and customer meaning

An audience is the broader group an organization intends to serve. A segment is a defined subset with a relevant shared characteristic or need. Positioning concerns what an offer means to a chosen audience relative to alternatives and why it may be chosen. A channel is a route through which people encounter, evaluate, buy, use, or hear from an offer. A channel's role may be discovery, consideration, conversion, retention, support, or learning; one channel can serve more than one role.

Acquisition concerns beginning a customer relationship. Conversion is a specified transition, such as sign-up to purchase. Retention concerns continued use or relationship over time. Awareness, preference, purchase, and continued use are different outcomes.

"We need more customers" names an outcome without locating the constraint. It might concern awareness, relevance, qualification, conversion, retention, capacity, or something else. "People do not understand the difference" may concern positioning, message clarity, product experience, or evidence; it does not establish which one.

## Outcomes, events, and evidence

An objective is the intended change. An outcome is the change of interest. An event is an observable action or state recorded by a system. A metric is a defined numerical measure. A key event is an event selected as especially meaningful for analysis. Attribution assigns credit for an outcome under a stated method. Incrementality asks what changed because of an intervention compared with a credible alternative.

An attributed conversion is not proof that a channel caused it. A change in a metric can reflect audience mix, seasonality, product changes, measurement changes, or other factors. "This campaign worked" may be a reported result, a provisional interpretation, or a causal claim; preserve which is known.

## Translate rough requests

| Rough expression | Possible precise meanings to keep open |
| --- | --- |
| "Find the right audience." | Define or examine a market, segment, need, behavior, or current customer pattern. |
| "Fix our funnel." | Locate a specified customer journey or transition; do not assume a linear funnel or a channel failure. |
| "Choose the best channel." | Compare channel roles against an objective, audience context, available evidence, and constraints. |
| "Improve conversion." | Clarify the transition, population, timeframe, and evidence before selecting a cause or tactic. |
| "Measure this campaign." | Define the outcome, events, decision, and limits of the available measurement. |
| "Prove this works." | Frame an evidence question; do not promise causality or fabricate results. |

These are possible interpretations rather than preset marketing plans. Preserve the user's market knowledge and uncertainty.

## Questions for expansion

Select questions that create meaningfully different directions:

- Whose behavior or experience matters, in what context, and relative to which alternatives?
- Is the problem about reaching people, relevance, their ability to evaluate the offer, a specific transition, or continued use?
- What role should each existing channel play, and what evidence supports that role?
- Which outcome would make a decision easier, and which observable events are suitable but incomplete proxies?
- What comparison would be needed before treating a pattern as evidence of impact?

A controlled single-variable test can be useful when isolating a specified change is the question. It is not a universal rule: multivariable tests, qualitative inquiry, observational analysis, or other approaches can be appropriate when requested and suited to the decision. Do not default to a funnel, framework, audience, channel, budget, metric, or experiment design.

## Across prompt modes

Clarify can specify the business question without converting a desired outcome into a causal diagnosis. Expand can offer routes organized around audience meaning, channel role, customer transition, or measurement uncertainty. Combine can retain the chosen outcome, customer context, and evidence limits even when its directions pull toward different channels. Prepare can distinguish a desired decision from proposed research, later execution, and the evidence that would make the decision assessable.

Shaping a marketing prompt does not authorize campaign activity, analytics changes, audience targeting, or spending. Keep forecasts and possible tactics marked as proposals until an underlying task authorizes work.

When a request crosses domains, name the handoff explicitly: marketing may frame the audience and outcome, copywriting may develop the message, product work may change the experience, and business operations may decide authority or resources.

## Evidence boundaries and sources

Keep reported activity, measured events, attributed results, causal findings, forecasts, and goals separate. Do not invent market size, customer insight, performance, benchmarks, spend, targeting, conversion rates, or campaign outcomes. Variants may propose different questions or hypotheses but must label them as such. Claims in copy require their own support; marketing plans do not make them true.
