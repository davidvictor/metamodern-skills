# Production glossary

Controlled vocabulary for prompts and briefs. Pick two to four terms per prompt clause. Each section ends with reference points for taste calibration only; never copy a named studio's work.

Sections 1 to 5 (print, paper, fabrication, textiles, screens) apply to brands with a physical product or a physical application under test. For world stills, start with section 0 and section 6.

## 0. Campaign-still vocabulary

- **One idea**: the single visual concept the image exists to show, stated in one sentence with no lists. Use when: every world still and mark tile; name it first.
- **Dominant form**: the one shape that owns the frame (a circle of light, a beam, a figure). Use when: composition must read at thumbnail size.
- **Scale contrast**: a tiny figure against a vast form, or one huge form against a small detail, stated literally. Use when: bluntness and editorial scale are in the register.
- **Negative space**: controlled darkness or emptiness named in words ("upper two-thirds empty wall"). Use when: the register is blunt rather than busy.
- **Light as subject**: the beam, circle, edge, or haze is what the image is of. Use when: identity is carried by light.
- **Motivated single source**: one light with a visible or implied cause (a round opening, an overhead key), no fill, shadow side falls to black. Use when: the image must read as intentional rather than lit.
- **Hard and soft as behavior**: hard gives an edged shadow; soft wraps. Say the behavior, not the gear.
- **Colored light**: brass-gold light, pale blue haze, ink black; warm key against cool ambient is the most reliable temperature move. Use when: color roles must appear without flat graphics.
- **Staged**: a built set or a real space arranged for the idea; one ground plane, one architectural fact, a count of objects, and what is absent. Use when: documentary realism would read as stock.
- **Figure as form**: an invented stand-in reduced to silhouette, scale, or gesture; half lit. Use when: the person is part of the composition, not a portrait.
- **Aperture, beam, edge, gap, haze**: the physical behaviors of light that can carry a mark's geometry. Use when: the mark appears as light.
- **Register in conduct words**: blunt, still, confident, slightly distant, business made visually alive. Use when: naming the locked register; never mood adjectives.
- **Finish as garnish**: one focal length, one depth phrase, at most one film character (medium-format look, fine grain, faint blue cast in shadows). Use when: pushing away from synthetic gloss without adding saturation.

Words that summon stock photography and are never used as the frame: photograph of, documentary, candid, natural light, soft window daylight, natural color, realistic, professional photo, modern office, team, conversation, beautiful, stunning, premium (bare), 8k, masterpiece.

Reference points: brand campaign stills where light is the subject and identity geometry appears through an aperture, beam, edge, gap, or haze.

## 1. Print processes

- **Letterpress**: type or plates pressed into paper, leaving a visible bite. Use when: cards, covers, invitations; signals care and weight.
- **Screen print**: ink pushed through a mesh, flat opaque color with slight edge softness. Use when: posters, textiles, thick single colors.
- **Offset litho**: smooth, precise, commercial. Use when: broadsheets, programs, reports.
- **Risograph**: soy ink, slight misregistration, visible grain. Use when: zines, quick editions, warm imperfection.
- **Foil**: metallic film pressed hot. Use when: one small hit of metal on matte stock.
- **Emboss and deboss**: raised or sunk form without ink. Use when: a mark as relief on cover stock.
- **Die cut**: shaped cut through the sheet. Use when: a mark or aperture that reveals a second layer.
- **Overprint**: two inks layered, a third color where they meet. Use when: color roles should visibly interact.

Reference points: Pentagram print work, Studio Dumbar posters, Anagrama stationery.

## 2. Paper and board

- **Uncoated**: matte, absorbent, warm. Use when: editorial, cream fields.
- **Coated gloss or silk**: sharp, reflective. Use when: photographic reproduction.
- **Cover stock 300 to 400 gsm**: rigid. Use when: covers, cards, one-sheets.
- **Newsprint**: thin, grey, fast. Use when: broadsheet specimens, disposable feel.
- **Kraft**: brown, fibrous. Use when: natural, unbleached register.
- **Greyboard**: thick, raw. Use when: exposed structure, die-cut layers.
- **Colored stock**: dyed through, not printed. Use when: a field color as material.

Reference points: GF Smith, Fedrigoni, Mohawk.

## 3. Signage and fabrication

- **Brushed brass**: soft directional sheen. Use when: warm metal accent.
- **Blackened steel**: dark, low reflectance. Use when: structural, quiet.
- **Powder coat**: uniform matte color on metal. Use when: color as an object.
- **Enamel**: glossy fired color, slight orange peel. Use when: signs, badges.
- **Neon or LED tube**: light as a line. Use when: the mark drawn in light.
- **Projected light**: image cast onto a surface. Use when: the mark on a wall or floor without an object.
- **Cut acrylic**: crisp edge, translucent or opaque. Use when: flat mark as a physical plane.
- **Vinyl**: applied color on glass or wall. Use when: environmental graphics.

Reference points: Pentagram environmental work, Cartlidge Levene wayfinding.

## 4. Textiles in brand environments

- **Wool felt**: dense, matte, absorbs light. Use when: warmth and quiet.
- **Linen**: visible weave, natural. Use when: editorial softness.
- **Canvas**: heavy, structural. Use when: totes, backdrops.
- **Embroidery on twill**: thread as the mark. Use when: badges, lanyards, patches.

Reference points: Kvadrat, Bode.

## 5. Screen and broadcast contexts

- **LED wall**: emissive, pixel structure at close range, deep black. Use when: stage and event.
- **Broadcast monitor**: calibrated, slight glass reflection. Use when: lower-thirds, studio.
- **Phone in hand**: small, glossy, environmental reflections. Use when: cover art in context.
- **Laptop on a table**: mid scale, ambient reflections. Use when: web or deck in context.
- **Lower third**: a band of type at the bottom of a broadcast frame. Use when: naming a speaker.

Reference points: live event design by Wolff Olins and DesignStudio.

## 6. Photographic light and lens

- **Soft top-down studio light**: even, catalog. Use when: flat lays, material tiles.
- **Raked side light**: steep angle, exaggerates texture. Use when: embossing, print bite, embroidery.
- **Soft frontal light**: even, flattering, low drama. Use when: portraits in a calm register.
- **Window light**: directional daylight, cool to neutral. Use when: rooms, editorial calm.
- **Practical light**: lamps in the scene doing the lighting. Use when: rooms at evening.
- **Golden hour**: low warm sun, long shadows. Use when: exterior context.
- **35 mm equivalent**: slightly wide, environmental. Use when: rooms and heroes.
- **50 mm equivalent**: natural. Use when: full-figure stand-ins.
- **85 mm equivalent**: compressed, flattering. Use when: portraits, material close-ups.
- **Medium-format feel**: smooth tonal range, compressed perspective. Use when: premium catalog register.
- **Slight grain**: film character. Use when: pushing away from synthetic gloss.

Reference points: Monocle photography, The Gentlewoman portraits, Apartamento interiors.

## 7. Motion grammar

- **Slow push**: camera moves toward the subject. Use when: revealing detail.
- **Slow pan**: camera turns across a scene. Use when: showing a room.
- **Slow reveal**: an element enters or uncovers. Use when: the mark's action continuing.
- **Hold**: no camera move, subtle ambient motion. Use when: material breathing, light shifting.
- **Loop**: end state matches start. Use when: web hero, ambient screens.

Rules: one move per clip; no cuts; no zoom bursts; no camera shake.

## 8. Type behavior

Describe behavior, not letterforms; exact type is set natively.

- **Wide bold grotesque**: heavy, extended sans. Use when: headline scale and bluntness.
- **Condensed grotesque**: narrow sans. Use when: dense information, labels.
- **Plain sans**: neutral. Use when: body, captions.
- **Monospace**: fixed width. Use when: data, technical labels.
- **Scale contrast**: one word very large, the rest small. Use when: hierarchy through size.
- **Weight contrast**: heavy against light in one line. Use when: hierarchy through weight.

Reference points: Bloomberg Businessweek, Uncensored CMO, MIT Technology Review.

## 9. Color as material

Say the material, never the code. Field, figure, accent.

- Near-black: ink black, blackened steel, black wool.
- Cream: unbleached cotton paper, ivory plaster, cream felt.
- Gold: brushed brass, warm foil, ochre pigment.
- Blues: powder-coated steel, dyed linen, light through frosted glass.
- Green: dark moss felt, oxidized copper.
- Brown: stained oak, saddle leather.

## How to use this glossary

Briefs: section 0 for world stills and mark tiles, 1 to 5 only for physical products and applications, 6 for photography, 7 for motion, 8 for type behavior, 9 for color.
Prompts: one material, one light, one lens per clause. A prompt with five processes produces a confused composite; a prompt with one process, one material, and one light produces a coherent photograph.
