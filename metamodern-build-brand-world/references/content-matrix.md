# Content matrix

Gate D generates the world systematically instead of from a list. Build a matrix of surfaces, contexts, and moments from the brief, generate one or two plates per cell, screen every cell against every lock, and show the four to six cells that span the widest range of surfaces.

## Method

1. **Surfaces**: the artifacts the brand actually ships, taken from the brief's application needs. Six to ten. Each surface is an image plate for Figma with clean space where type, the lockup, and real portraits will go. Surfaces are never mockups of objects (a printed sheet on a table, a badge, a poster in a corridor); those read as skeuomorphism and are not generated.
2. **Contexts**: the two or three places the locked world exists, taken from the Gate B and Gate C locks (for example, the locked floor and the locked wall, plus one live or public context if the brand has one).
3. **Moments**: two lighting or occasion states from the locked photography, for example studio dark and live event.
4. Cells are surface by context by moment. Prune cells that cannot occur.
5. Every cell prompt embeds the style-lock elements by placeholder and carries no text at all; words are set natively.
6. Keep the mark at scene scale in every cell. A close-range detail in which the mark is the subject fails as a class: light at close range renders as material.
7. Screen all cells as they land and send passing plates to the director as they land. Show the widest spread. Repeat the hand test as a with-and-without pair on one cell.

## Example, brand-neutral

Surfaces: hero artifact plate (square); wide graphic plate (16:9) with clean space for a title; portrait treatment plate with a stand-in; social quote plate with an empty center; tall hero plate (4:5); presentation title plate; live or public context plate; two-person plate.

Contexts: the locked room; the locked wall; the public stage.

Moments: studio dark; live event.

Cells kept: hero plate in the room; portrait treatment on the wall; social quote on the wall with the center empty; tall hero in the room; presentation title with a figure crossing the wall; live event with the mark's light as the only light and an audience in darkness; two-person plate on the wall. Discarded as a class: any close-range detail with the mark as subject.
