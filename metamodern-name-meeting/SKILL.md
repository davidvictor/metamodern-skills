# Metamodern Name Meeting

## Purpose

Make a meeting title feel like an implausibly important upload. Be sincere about the work and self aware about the ordinary drama of doing it together. The main title reveals the real premise; the parenthetical reframes that premise as content.

## Method

1. Infer the purpose, participants, stakes, tension, and hoped for outcome from the user's description or the actual event. If information is sparse, use a recognizable meeting type and avoid inventing facts or outcomes.
2. Write an honest premise as a question, attempted feat, plain update, or modest confession. Prefer specific constraints and human language over generic corporate wording.
3. Add a compact parenthetical borrowed from the conventions of video uploads, music releases, live performances, challenge videos, reaction videos, reviews, or documentaries. Make it the punchline, rather than a redundant category label.
4. Keep the meeting legible to attendees. Aim humor at the shared situation and process, not at someone's competence or identity. Do not promise that a conflict will resolve or that a launch will succeed before the meeting occurs.
5. Use no dashes in the calendar title. Write a flowing phrase followed by parentheses. Follow more specific user style instructions when given.

## Title vocabulary

| Situation | Premise | Parenthetical |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Update | Say plainly who is reporting what | (Official Audio), (Live From the Conference Room), (Full Performance) |
| Decision | Ask the unresolved question | (INTENSE), (Final Round), (Uncut) |
| Planning | Turn the real constraints into a challenge | (Realistic Results), (Impossible Challenge), (Day One) |
| Retrospective | Admit the awkward history | (Director's Commentary), (Blooper Reel), (Reunion Special) |
| Review | Invite an honest reaction | (First Reactions), (Honest Review), (Extended Cut) |
| Repeat conversation | Name the sequel | (Remastered 2026), (Part 3), (Previously on This Team) |

Rotate formats across a calendar. Let a mundane tag become funny through disproportionate prestige, and let a dramatic tag exaggerate a small but real tension. A random parenthetical detached from the premise is weaker than one that changes how the whole title reads.

## Calibration

These are invented meeting titles, not real video titles:

* How Far Can We Get With Half the Budget We Were Promised? (Realistic Results)
* September Product Team Updates (Official Audio)
* Customer Support LIVE From the Conference Room (Full Performance)
* Can Sarah and Ben Agree on a Plan in 30 Minutes? (INTENSE)
* Q2 Blooper Reel (Retrospective)
* We Need to Decide What “Soon” Means (Extended Cut)
* The Launch Date Discussion We Had Last Week (Remastered 2026)

The first five follow the supplied visual references and their cadence. Use actual colleagues' names only when supplied or clearly appropriate for the invitation.

## Return

For one meeting, give one strongest copy ready title and two or three alternatives with different comic angles. For a naming system or batch, give a short rule and a varied set of titles. Explain the joke only when asked. If asked to rename actual calendar events, inspect their purposes and make only the authorized edits.

When the user asks for real YouTube examples or current inspiration, research actual titles, cite sources, and clearly distinguish those from invented meeting names. Routine naming does not require research.
